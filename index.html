Angaraag Mahanta (born 24 November 1975), known professionally as Papon, is an Indian playback singer, composer, songwriter and record producer from Assam. His work combines Assamese folk and devotional traditions with Indian classical music, electronic production, pop, rock, Sufi music and ghazal. He has recorded in Assamese, Hindi, Bengali, Tamil, Kannada, Marathi, Urdu, Mising, Bodo, Tiwa, Punjabi and Odia.[21][26]

Born into a musical family, he is the son of Assamese folk singers Khagen Mahanta and Archana Mahanta. His Hindi-film work includes “Jiyein Kyun”, “Kyon”, “Moh Moh Ke Dhaage”, “Humnava” and “Bulleya”.[3][6]

Career

Early recordings and regional albums

Papon made his first lead recording in 1988, singing the Assamese song “Gaantu Je Gaai Asila” for the album Aamiu Gao Gaan as a duet with Lima Das.[2] In 1998, he recorded “Nasaba Sokule” with Zubeen Garg for Snigdha Junak. His first solo album, Jonaaki Raati (also styled Junaki Raati), followed in 2004; he subsequently released original and folk-based Assamese projects including Rong Phool, Sinaaki Osinaaki, Gomseng, Phagunar Gaan and Phoolseng.[3][5]

Hindi films and national recognition

His first Hindi-film song was “Sajna Baware” (also listed as “Sajana”) from Let’s Enjoy (2004). In a Reuters interview, Papon said that he sang, wrote and produced the track, which was later co-produced by MIDIval Punditz.[3] This corrects the older claim that “Om Mantra” from Strings – Bound by Faith (2006) was his Bollywood debut.[4]

“Jiyein Kyun” from Dum Maaro Dum (2011) became his first major Hindi-film success. It was followed by songs including “Kyon” from Barfi! (2012), “Kaun Mera” from Special 26 (2013), “Lakeerein” from Kya Dilli Kya Lahore (2014), “Moh Moh Ke Dhaage” from Dum Laga Ke Haisha (2015), “Humnava” from Hamari Adhuri Kahani (2015), “Bulleya” from Sultan (2016), “Labon Ka Karobaar” from Befikre (2016), “Tu Chale Toh” from Qarib Qarib Singlle (2017), “Heartquake” from Karwaan (2018) and “Baba Bolta Hain Bas Ho Gaya” from Sanju (2018).[6][30]

In 2013 he composed and sang “Jajabor”, the theme song for MTV Roadies X: Battle for Glory. In 2014 he made his acting debut as a science professor in the Assamese film Rodor Sithi. A seven-part documentary web series, Hometown Heroes, traced his musical development and was released in 2016.[8][10][7]

Later projects and Cannes

During the late 2010s and 2020s, Papon continued to release film, regional and independent music. His projects included Notun Puhor (2017), Börgeet – Ghôxaa, Vol. 1 (2019), the Axone soundtrack (2020), Unacademy Unwind (2023), songs and an on-screen musical appearance in Metro... In Dino (2025), and the soundtrack of Tu Meri Poori Kahani (2025).[6][22][28]

In 2022, he attended the Cannes Film Festival as a cultural ambassador of Assam and represented the state’s culture and cinema at the India Pavilion. Teasers for two projects produced by him—The Land of the Sacred Beats and The Mystical Brahmaputra – A Musical Story—were presented during the festival.[13][14]

In 2026, he released the four-track ghazal EP Mehfilz with Papon – Irshad (From the Living Room), written with lyricist Irshad Kamil, and expanded the concept with further “Sar-e-Shaam” releases. The same year, “Kuni Kuni” marked his Odia recording debut, adding Odia to his recorded-language catalogue.[20][21][31][32]

Musical style and collaborations

Papon’s work draws on Bihu, Borgeet and other Assamese musical traditions, alongside Indian classical music, ghazal, Sufi music, electronic production, rock and trip hop. His collaborations include work with MIDIval Punditz, Pritam, Shantanu Moitra, Vishal–Shekhar, Amit Trivedi, Anu Malik, Karsh Kale, Rabbi Shergill, Harshdeep Kaur, Bickram Ghosh, Rachel Sermanni and Irshad Kamil. He has also appeared on Coke Studio @ MTV, The Dewarists, MTV Unplugged and Bangladesh’s Wind of Change.[3][6][20]

Former live ensemble

In 2007, Papon formed the folk-fusion live group Papon and the East India Company. The ensemble combined Assamese folk and Indian classical elements with rock, blues, trip hop and live electronics. In a 2016 interview, Papon said that changing personnel meant the act was no longer called East India Company and that later performances had become “Papon’s set”. The former name is therefore retained here only as historical career information, not as a current affiliation.[11]

Paponists

Papon’s fan community is known as the Paponists. Media profiles have reported that members of the community organised cleanliness campaigns, charity work and other social initiatives inspired by messages shared through his music and public appearances.[12]

Philanthropy and environmental work

Papon and Shweta Mishra Mahanta established the Earthful Foundation in March 2018. Its stated focus areas include waste management, sustainable livelihoods, health, nutrition, environmental awareness and disaster response.[15]

In 2014, Papon and Vishal Dadlani spearheaded the #FORASSAM fundraising concert in Delhi for people affected by floods in Assam. The initiative returned during the 2019 floods, alongside fundraising and relief work by volunteers often described in coverage as the “Papon Army”. Earthful Foundation’s later work included the Xökaah relief initiative and the distribution of health and hygiene kits in rural Assam during the COVID-19 pandemic.[17][18][16]

Personal life and education

Papon was raised in a musical household by Khagen Mahanta and Archana Mahanta. After school in Guwahati, he moved to Delhi. He initially explored architecture, enrolled for English studies at Ramjas College and later attended Motilal Nehru College, but left university to pursue music. He lives in Mumbai with his wife, Shweta Mishra Mahanta, and their children.[19][15]
