Angaraag Mahanta (born 24 November 1975), known professionally as Papon, is an Indian playback singer, composer, songwriter and record producer from Assam. He works across Assamese folk and devotional traditions, Indian classical music, pop, rock, electronica, ghazal, Sufi and film music. Public sources document recordings in Assamese, Hindi, Bengali, Tamil, Kannada, Marathi, Urdu, Mising, Bodo, Tiwa, Punjabi and Odia.[22]

He is the son of Assamese singers Khagen Mahanta and Archana Mahanta. His Hindi-film songs include “Jiyein Kyun”, “Kyon”, “Kaun Mera”, “Moh Moh Ke Dhaage”, “Humnava”, “Bulleya”, “Labon Ka Karobaar”, “Chaav Laaga”, “Nazar Teri Toofan” and several recordings for Metro... In Dino. His work also includes Assamese albums, independent releases, television music formats, film acting, production projects and social initiatives.

Career

Papon's first official lead recording was the Assamese song “Gaantu Je Gaai Asila” for the 1988 album Aamiu Gao Gaan, recorded as a duet with Dr Lima Das.[2] In 1998 he recorded “Nasaba Sokule” with Zubeen Garg for Snigdha Junak. His first solo Assamese album, Jonaaki Raati / Junaki Raati, belongs to his 2004–05 breakthrough period; later profiles distinguish between the original release and its Guwahati launch.[4]

His Hindi-film chronology begins earlier than the current public article historically suggested. In a Reuters interview, Papon described “Sajna Baware” from Let's Enjoy as a song he sang, wrote and produced before it was co-produced by MIDIval Punditz, and he described “Jiyein Kyun” from Dum Maaro Dum (2011) as his first major Bollywood song.[3]

Production and Cannes projects

During his 2022 Cannes visit as a cultural ambassador of Assam, Papon presented teasers for two projects associated with him: The Land of the Sacred Beats and The Mystical Brahmaputra – A Musical Story.[7][8]

Former live ensemble

In 2007, Papon formed the folk-fusion live group Papon and the East India Company. The act combined Assamese folk and Indian classical influences with rock, blues, trip hop and electronic elements. Papon later said that changing personnel meant the act was no longer called East India Company and that later performances had become “Papon's set”; the name is therefore retained here only as historical context.[5]

Personal life and education

Papon grew up in Guwahati in a musical household. After school, he moved to Delhi, initially explored architecture and later enrolled in English studies at the University of Delhi, spending time at Ramjas College and Motilal Nehru College before pursuing music full-time.[16] His father, Khagen Mahanta, died in June 2014. Papon lives in Mumbai with his wife, Shweta Mishra Mahanta, and their children.

Collaborations

Papon has worked with composers, producers, lyricists and performers including MIDIval Punditz, Pritam, Shantanu Moitra, Vishal–Shekhar, Amit Trivedi, Anu Malik, Karsh Kale, Rabbi Shergill, Harshdeep Kaur, Bickram Ghosh, Shreya Ghoshal, Sunidhi Chauhan, Irshad Kamil and others. His collaborative and session work has appeared on Coke Studio @ MTV, The Dewarists, MTV Unplugged, Wind of Change and multiple film soundtracks.

Paponists

Papon's fan community is known as the Paponists. Hindustan Times reported that members of the community had organised cleanliness drives and other socially oriented activities inspired by messages in his public appearances.[6]

Philanthropy and environmental work

Papon and Shweta Mishra Mahanta established Earthful Foundation in 2018. The organisation describes its areas of work as including environmental conservation, sustainable livelihoods, health, nutrition and disaster response.[9]

In 2014, Papon and Vishal Dadlani spearheaded the #FORASSAM fundraising concert in Delhi for people affected by Assam floods.[10] Relief and rehabilitation work involving Papon and volunteers associated with his initiatives was again reported during the 2019 Assam floods.[11] Earthful Foundation later carried out rural Assam COVID-relief work and the Xokaah initiative.[12]
